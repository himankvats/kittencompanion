# Stakeholder Register

**Project:** Kitten Companion
**Phase:** 1 — Initiation & Discovery
**Status:** Draft

---

## Purpose

A focused list of parties with a stake in this project's success, failure, or visibility — and the project's engagement approach with each. The register is short because the project is solo and pre-deployment. Many entries that would appear on a real product's stakeholder register (legal, finance, regulatory bodies, investors, board) are absent because they don't exist for this project.

Influence is scored High/Medium/Low against the project's go/no-go decision and execution. Interest is what each party stands to gain or lose. Engagement is the practical approach.

## Notes on specific entries

**Entry 1 (Engineer as stakeholder).** Including yourself in your own stakeholder register is slightly unusual. It's here because the project has acknowledged dual purpose — a real attempt at a useful tool, and a portfolio artifact — and pretending the engineer's career interests aren't a stakeholder would make the document less honest. Naming it lets later decisions be evaluated against it explicitly: "am I making this call because it serves users, or because it serves the portfolio?"

**Entry 3 (Vets).** Vets appear as secondary beneficiaries, not primary users. This matters: a register that listed vets with high influence would imply the project's success depends on vet adoption, which isn't the case. The vet summary is a value-add. The primary mechanism works without vets engaging at all. Veterinarian outreach during Phase 1 is for validation, not for buy-in.

**Entry 5 (Recruiters and hiring managers).** The audience for the project's portfolio purpose. They never interact with the project except through its public artifacts. Their influence is high because the portfolio purpose is one of two reasons the project exists, but the engagement is entirely indirect — there's no recruiter-facing surface to design.

**Entry 6 (Professional obligations).** Abstracted deliberately. The pet project domain was chosen specifically to avoid any overlap with the engineer's day-job work. The engagement is mostly negative space — the project's job is to remain clearly unrelated to the engineer's professional domain. This entry exists in the register because it has real influence on the project's existence, not because there's an active stakeholder to engage.

**Entry 10 (Scooter).** Listed with low formal influence because a cat can't influence project decisions, but flagged because the project's existence cannot be allowed to displace day-to-day responsibility for an actual living animal. A check against the project becoming an excuse to *think* about being a good owner rather than *being* one.

## What is NOT on this register, and why

A real product would include:
- **Legal counsel** — not relevant for a pre-deployment portfolio project; would be engaged if/when the project commercializes
- **Investors / funders** — none; project has no commercial intent
- **Regulators** — the project deliberately stays out of veterinary medical advice, the only regulatory surface that could apply
- **App store gatekeepers** — relevant at deployment time, not Phase 1
- **Co-founders, team members** — none; solo project
- **Partner organizations (shelters, retailers, manufacturers)** — possible Phase 3+ but premature to register

Each of these may appear in future revisions if the project's scope or stage changes.
